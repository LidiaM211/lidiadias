One thing I set as a goal when creating this Substack was to bring more examples of how good stories sell, no matter how <i>boring</i> the industry or type of product may seem. People often think that storytelling is reserved for big, sexy consumer brands, rather than less “exciting” products like enterprise software or services.

The internet is full of incredible DTC brand stories, but what about the underdogs? Sure, Nike, IKEA, Starbucks: they all have the means and the budget to take an insight and build an engaging story around it. But can we take that to a smaller scale? Of course we can.

Social media, in particular, has been a great growth engine for these smaller brands. A TikTok account, a little bit of charisma, and a willingness to put yourself out there have been fuelling the growth of small brands. That was the case with Pleo.

Pleo was founded in 2015 in Copenhagen and has rapidly expanded since then, with offices now in London, Berlin, Stockholm, Madrid, Lisbon, Paris, Amsterdam and Chennai. Their product is expense management software, not exciting at all. But Pleo built a brand around how people deal with money at work: they talk about the role of finance teams in a company’s growth, and the time wasted managing company cards, receipts, reimbursements and accounting reconciliation, bringing people to their content because they work in finance or run a business, and not because they’re currently searching for an expense software.

Pleo organizes its content strategy around three or four core narratives, such as AI in Finance, the evolving role of the CFO, or cash flow management. Rather than starting with product features, these narratives are built around the challenges, interests, and priorities of its target personas. From there, each narrative is developed across multiple formats and touchpoints, including articles, videos, reports, campaigns, emails, and other content. Pleo’s strategy is not built around producing more content. Instead, the company creates a small number of narratives and turns them into multiple formats and touchpoints.

Example: [The CFO’s first 90 days: A practical playbook for strategic onboarding](https://blog.pleo.io/en/practical-playbook-for-strategic-onboarding)

Pleo starts by earning the audience’s attention before trying to build buying intent. By consistently creating content around its audience’s interests, questions, and problems, the brand builds a broader mental association: not just “expense management = Pleo”, but “modern finance = Pleo”.

This has been part of the strategy from the beginning. Over time, Pleo built an extensive library of top-of-funnel content around topics such as mileage rates, invoicing, finance processes, reimbursements, and invoice management.

Long before LLMs changed how people search for information, Pleo was focused on being the answer to almost any finance-related question its audience might type into Google. That gave the brand a significant head start in SEO and helped build the organic authority that continues to fuel its visibility today.

As Alejandro Salse Batán, Pleo’s Head of Brand and Content Marketing, explains, “most people still find Pleo’s content via Google,” making search not only one of the company’s biggest discovery channels, but also one of its strongest lead generators.

Localization has also played an important role in that strategy. When Pleo entered the UK, for example, it did not simply translate content created for other markets. It developed content specifically for British businesses, covering local regulations, legislation, and finance processes. The topic might be the same, but the answer was adapted to the reality of the market.

Example: [European Finance Index: UK businesses are done experimenting with AI. The receipts prove it](https://blog.pleo.io/en/26q1-euro-finance-index-uk)

Between May 2024 and December 2025, Pleo’s SEO performance reportedly delivered:

- 61% growth in organic traffic;
- 229% growth in the estimated value of that traffic;
- 418% growth in keywords ranking in positions 1–3;
- 171% growth in keywords ranking in the top 10.

But the most interesting number is not the traffic growth itself. It is the fact that the estimated value of that traffic grew far faster than its volume. That suggests that Pleo is not only attracting more people, but also attracting audiences with a stronger connection to the problems Pleo can actually solve, becoming a true growth engine.

All the content efforts are especially effective because Pleo has a distinctive brand voice. Its content is informal, visual and often humorous, using small frustrations of work as creative material. Pleo does not pretend expense management is exciting, but it picks the related frustrations and gives them a human lens, so the audience feels understood. Also, they reframe the way the audience sees itself. They position finance teams as people who are helping the business grow, and the product is just a tool that removes the administrative work getting in the way.

Watch: [Onboarding with Pleo: SILICON](https://lnkd.in/p/eh6yJGdm)

For me, the best part of Pleo’s content strategy is that it does not try to make expense management interesting; it makes people dealing with it interesting by building content around their challenges, ambitions and identity. Pleo is now more than just another product they discover: it’s a brand that they know, understand and trust.
